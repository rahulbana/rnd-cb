The Quiet Rise of Small Language Models

For the past few years, the story of artificial intelligence has been a story
about size. Bigger models, trained on more data, with more parameters, kept
setting new records. But a quieter trend has been building alongside the race
for scale: small language models, or SLMs, that run on laptops, phones, and
even microcontrollers.

Small language models typically range from a few hundred million to a few
billion parameters, compared to the hundreds of billions in frontier systems.
What they give up in raw breadth they often make up for in speed, cost, and
privacy. Because they can run locally, sensitive data never has to leave the
device. Because they are small, they respond almost instantly and cost very
little to operate.

The techniques that make these models punch above their weight are worth
understanding. Distillation lets a small "student" model learn from a larger
"teacher," capturing much of its capability in a fraction of the size.
Quantization shrinks the numerical precision of a model's weights, cutting
memory use dramatically with only a small hit to quality. And careful curation
of training data has shown that a smaller model trained on cleaner, more
relevant examples can outperform a larger model trained on noisy data.

There are real trade-offs. Small models still struggle with tasks that require
broad world knowledge or long chains of reasoning. They are easier to push off
track and can be more brittle outside their training distribution. For many
production uses, teams now combine approaches: a small model handles the common,
latency-sensitive cases on-device, and escalates to a larger model in the cloud
only when needed.

The implications go beyond engineering. When capable models can run anywhere,
the economics of AI change. Startups can ship features without huge inference
bills. Regulated industries can keep data on-premises. And entirely new
categories of always-on, private assistants become possible. The age of giant
models is not ending, but it is gaining a fast, frugal, and surprisingly
capable companion.
