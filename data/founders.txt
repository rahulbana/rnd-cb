Ada Lovelace worked with Charles Babbage on the Analytical Engine in the
1840s. Babbage designed the Analytical Engine, a mechanical general-purpose
computer, in London. Ada Lovelace wrote the first published algorithm intended
to be carried out by the Analytical Engine, which is why she is often called
the first computer programmer.

Alan Turing later founded the field of theoretical computer science. Turing
worked at Bletchley Park during World War II, where he helped build the Bombe,
a machine used to break the Enigma cipher used by the German military. Turing
also proposed the Turing Test as a way to judge machine intelligence.

Grace Hopper was an American computer scientist who worked on the Harvard Mark
I computer. Hopper invented one of the first compilers and contributed to the
development of COBOL, a programming language widely used in business systems.
The Harvard Mark I was built by IBM and installed at Harvard University.
